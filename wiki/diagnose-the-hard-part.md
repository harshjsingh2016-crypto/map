# Diagnose the hard part

The selection rule for content work, and the correction to the instinct that produces most bad
AI output: three jobs that all end in written words are not one job. Before choosing a tool,
name **what is hard about the brief**. Not what it produces, not who asked for it, not how
urgent it is. The tool follows the diagnosis, and choosing without one means choosing by output
format, which is the wrong axis.

Three briefs make the point. A partner pitch deck where the person writing it already knows the
commission terms, the numbers and the argument — nothing is missing from her head, only the
form a room of skimming readers will sit through. A pile of phone notes where every fact is real
and present but unstructured, inconsistently formatted, and ambiguous in at least one place. A
week of social captions where no single right answer exists and the difficulty is producing
consistent voice repeatedly. Structure, substance, style.

What makes this a real distinction rather than a taxonomy is that the three fail differently, and
the difference is commercial. A wrong number on a partner deck loses the deal, because the person
across the table is negotiating against that number. Invented copy on a public page misleads
customers, which is a liability rather than a lost sale. Captions in the wrong voice are the
worst of the three to catch, because nothing breaks: the posts go out, they are scrolled past,
and the enquiries simply never arrive. No error message is generated anywhere.

So the diagnosis is not really about which tool writes best. It is about which failure you are
choosing to spend your review time defending against. A tool optimised for speed and structure
will fill a gap in your brief rather than flag it. A tool optimised for careful reading will not
produce a deck. Picking one is accepting its characteristic failure and budgeting the proofread
that covers it.

One honest caveat about the frame itself. The structure / substance / style naming comes from a
section bookmark on the lecture recording. The instructor never presents it as a named framework
and the class notes carry no trace of the section at all. The idea is load-bearing; the label is
not something to cite.
