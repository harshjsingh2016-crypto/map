# Finished-looking is not checked

The sentence the content-creation lecture rests on, and the one worth carrying out of it:
**all three tools produce finished-looking content, and none of them checks its own facts.**

The claim is easy to misread as "AI output is unreliable", which is both weaker and less useful.
The real point is that polish and accuracy are produced by two different processes, and only one
of them is running. Everything comes back looking like a finished draft, because looking finished
is precisely what these tools are built to do. Whether the figures in it are the figures you
supplied is a question no one in the loop has asked, and no part of the output signals the
difference. There is no confidence marker, no gap where a number should have been, no visible
seam between what you gave it and what it filled in.

That is why fluency is the specific hazard rather than the reassurance it feels like. A draft that
reads badly gets read carefully. A draft that reads well gets skimmed and shipped, which means the
better the tool gets at the surface, the more of the checking burden quietly moves onto you.

Volume sharpens it. A batch of twenty social posts that all sound right is twenty pieces of
unchecked copy, and the per-piece cost of reading them is what makes the pass feel skippable
exactly when there is most to miss. This is why the proofread is drawn as its own step in the
Canva pipeline rather than assumed: naming it as a step is the only thing that stops it being
absorbed into the generation that precedes it.

So the skill has two halves and they are equally weighted. Match the tool to the job, then read
every draft before it ships. The second half is where people economise, because it looks like
overhead attached to a task that was supposed to be automated.

The time accounting explains why it is not overhead. You are not saving the writing time and
keeping the reviewing time. You are trading a long writing pass for a short generation plus a real
review pass, and the total still comes out far ahead. The review is not a tax on the saving. It is
the part of the job that stayed yours, and the reason the saving is worth anything.
